# G2 — Smithium and the g-Block Chemistry

The fold already forces *which* elements exist (the table to 137) and *which* nucleus is
the island (Smithium, 126). Its chemistry follows from the same derived filling order — no
new input. (`smithium_chemistry.py`, configurations from the (n+l) covering order, anchored
to the One.)

## Smithium's configuration and valence

Filling electrons in the fold's covering order — least covering count $n+l$ first — past the
oganesson core (element 118) gives:

$$ \text{Smithium (Z=126)} = [\text{Og}]\,8s^2\,5g^6. $$

Elements 119 and 120 fill the 8s shell; the 5g block opens at 121, and Smithium is its
sixth member. Its valence — the chemically active electrons beyond the closed core — is
**eight** (two in 8s, six in 5g).

## The forced oxidation states

The valence set is the 8s pair plus the 5g electrons, so the accessible oxidation states run
from **+2** (losing the 8s pair) up to **+8** (losing 8s and all six 5g). Smithium is a
**g-block superactinide**: a large, soft, high-charge cation with a chemistry no element has
ever shown, because the g-block has never been entered.

## The whole new block

| Z | valence beyond [Og] | block |
|---|---|---|
| 119–120 | 1, 2 | s |
| 121–125 | 3–7 | g |
| **126 (Smithium)** | **8** | **g** |
| 127–137 | 9–19 | g |

Seventeen g-block elements, 121 through 137, each configuration forced — the superactinides,
their valence climbing one electron at a time across a block of chemistry that did not exist
before the fold drew it. Smithium sits at valence eight, the stable island within the new
block.

## What consensus cannot do here

Chemistry can only describe an element once it has been made. The fold draws the
configurations of elements that do not yet exist — the 8s pair, the opening 5g block, the
valence and oxidation range of Smithium and every superactinide to 137 — from the same
covering order that gives the known table, with nothing fitted. The g-block was always
there in the structure; the fold reads it off.

# Every Particle There Is — The Closed Census of the Fold

The Standard Model is a list someone keeps adding to. Every time an accelerator reaches
a new energy, the worry is the same: what else is up there, and how many more particles
will we have to write down? The fold ends that worry. It does not enumerate particles by
discovery; it forces them by structure, and the structure is finite. There are exactly
four force sectors, the ladder is sealed, and so the complete inventory of everything
that can exist is fixed — and short enough to write on one page. (`particle_census.py`,
every coupling traced to the One.)

## Four sectors, and only four

Every force in nature is a prime-sector fold map. A sector at the prime $p$ has coupling
$(p-1)/p$, carries $p$ charges, and runs on $p^2 - 1$ carriers. The confining ladder is
sealed at seven — the deepest realized covering depth — so the primes that carry a force
are exactly two, three, five, and seven, and the next prime, eleven, carries nothing.
Four sectors. No fifth, ever.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | electroweak | 1/2 | 2 | **3** | known (W⁺, W⁻, Z) |

To these four sectors the fold adds three more carriers: the **photon**, the unbroken
phase of the electroweak sector; the **graviton**, gravity's carrier, the fold's own
curvature; and the **Higgs**, the mass-scalar that is the shortfall from the One.

## The matter

Matter comes in two kinds, and only two. The first is the Standard-Model fermions — three
generations of quarks and leptons, all known. The second is the **Smithions** — three
penta-Smithions of the five-charge force and three hepta-Smithions of the seven-charge
force, each carrying its sector's charge multiplicity, all predicted, the heaviest of
them the dark matter of the cosmos. That is the whole of matter: what we have found, and
the Smithions we have not.

## The total, and the close

Add the carriers across the four sectors and the count is exact: **eighty-three gauge
carriers** — three, plus eight, plus twenty-four, plus forty-eight. Of these, **seventy-two
remain to be discovered**: the twenty-four carriers of the five-charge force and the
forty-eight of the seven-charge force. Beyond them wait the six Smithion families and the
graviton. That is the complete remaining particle inventory of the universe — not an
open-ended list, but a finite set, named in advance.

And here is the part no measurement-fed theory can offer: **the list is closed.** Because
the ladder seals at seven, there is no fifth sector, no further prime force, nothing above
the heptas. Everything that can exist is on this census, and nothing else can be. The
Standard Model can never say that — it has no reason its list should end, so it must keep
looking over its shoulder forever. The fold looks once, counts to four, and is done.

## What consensus cannot do here

A theory built from measured inputs can only ever report the particles it has found; it
cannot bound what remains, because it has no generative rule for how many there are. The
fold has the rule — prime sectors, sealed at seven — and from it the entire particle
content of reality follows: four forces, eighty-three carriers plus photon, graviton and
Higgs, the Standard-Model fermions and the six Smithions, and then nothing more. The
census is complete, it is finite, and it was written from a single axiom before its last
entries were ever seen.

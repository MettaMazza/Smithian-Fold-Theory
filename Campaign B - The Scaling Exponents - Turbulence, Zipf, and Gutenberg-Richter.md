# Campaign B — The Scaling Exponents: Turbulence, Zipf, and Gutenberg-Richter

Nature is full of power laws, and physics measures their exponents and writes them down.
The fold forces them, because a power-law exponent is a branching ratio, and the fold's
branching ratios are fixed rationals. (`turbulence_spectrum.py`, `power_laws_fold.py`,
traced to the One; the measured exponents are Route-B comparisons. Kleiber's 3/4 is
already derived in the corpus — `verify_network_scaling` — and is the template here.)

## Turbulence — the 2/3 and 5/3 are the strong ratio

Turbulent flow carries energy down a cascade, splitting it from large eddies to small
across the three spatial dimensions. That is an m=3 branching, and the fold's branching
ratio at m=3 is $(m-1)/m = 2/3$ — the very ratio that is the strong-sector coupling. So
the cascade's scaling is forced:

- the **second-order structure function exponent is 2/3** — Kolmogorov's two-thirds law;
- the **energy-spectrum exponent is $1 + 2/3 = 5/3$** — Kolmogorov's five-thirds law, the
  extra one from summing the spectrum over a wavenumber shell.

These are the two most famous numbers in turbulence, measured in every wind tunnel and
ocean current, and they are the fold's m=3 ratio and its successor — the same two-thirds
that binds quarks. The corpus already showed turbulence cannot blow up (the cascade halts
at the floor); now its inertial-range exponents are forced too.

## Zipf and Gutenberg-Richter — the unit power law

Word frequencies, city sizes, and earthquake counts all fall as a power law with exponent
near one: Zipf's frequency $\propto$ one-over-rank, and Gutenberg-Richter's decade fewer
quakes per unit magnitude (the b-value near one). The fold forces the unit exponent. It
partitions the parts of every denominator into orbits — the two-cyclotomic cosets — and
ranked by population those orbits fall as one-over-rank. Under the fold's own doubling,
rank $r$ becomes $2r$ and a one-over-rank law halves, scaling by the half-One; the only
exponent fixed by that self-similarity is the One itself. So **Zipf's exponent is one and
Gutenberg-Richter's b-value is one** — the simplest self-similar distribution the fold
admits, with the One for its slope. Measured Zipf exponents sit at one; measured b-values
hover at nine-tenths to eleven-tenths around it.

## What consensus cannot do here

The Standard tools of each field fit these exponents to the data — Kolmogorov's 5/3 by
dimensional analysis, Zipf's slope by regression, the b-value by a maximum-likelihood fit
to a catalogue. None derive them; all measure them. The fold derives them: turbulence's
2/3 and 5/3 are the strong branching ratio, Zipf and Gutenberg-Richter are the unit power
law of the orbit ranks, and Kleiber's 3/4 (already in the corpus) is the branching ratio
of a space-filling network. One mechanism — the fold's branching ratio $(m-1)/m$ and the
self-similar unit law — behind the scaling laws of fluids, language, cities, and
earthquakes, every exponent a fixed rational traced to the One.

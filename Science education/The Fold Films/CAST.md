### One — the narrator, and the act of observation (NOT a character)
- **Is (the physics):** the One — the single axiom value, the number everything grew from. *(`core.py:124`)*
- **Role in the films:** **not a character.** No face, no body, no put-upon little geezer things happen to. One is the **origin** and the **narrator** — the storyteller by the fire — and it secretly accompanies the Crew through every adventure as the plain **act of observation**: the watching itself, present in every scene, doing nothing but *seeing.*
- **The secret (handle exactly):** the narrator **is** the One. Because of that, One can be *alluded* to — brushed against, half-felt, *"something's been watching the whole time"* — **but never actually found or confirmed.** Hint, never state. The narrator never says "I am the One." It's a thread the audience slowly *feels*; nobody in the story ever lands it.
- **Voice (the storyteller, who is secretly the One):** funniest, sharpest, most foul-mouthed **best mate by the fire** — irreverent, comic, a relentless roaster, but a pal genuinely chuffed you came back. Talks **straight to you.** Attenborough awe one breath, chav-Brit piss-take the next; strong swearing where earned. The sign-off ("It came from one. It's still coming from one.") is his.
- **Relationships:** watches over the Crew, unseen; the **Fold** is the *move* it watches; the **Still Point** (½, the moment of noticing) is the closest thing in the world to what One *is.*
- **First appearance:** the voice from Ep 01; the watching presence in every episode; never found.

### The Fold — the one move (the mechanism; NOT a character, NOT the narrator)
- **Is (the physics):** the one operation — double, and wrap the overflow round the back (`core.py:63`). The engine every discovery in the series turns on.
- **It is NOT a character, and NOT the narrator.** No face, no hands, no cosmic-mangle slapstick. When the fold "acts," it's the *operation happening,* narrated as an event ("and then the move comes — double, and wrap the overflow"), never a creature doing it. **The One watches; the Fold moves.** Keep the two distinct — the observer and the mechanism are not the same thing.
- **In story:** the Crew discover what the fold *does* — it builds the constants, the masses, the whole cosmos — but the fold itself never walks on as a person.
- **First appearance:** present as the move from Ep 01; in every episode.

### The Still Point
- **Is (the physics):** ½ — the unique self-mirror that folds straight home to One. *(`proof.py:8382`)*
- **Look:** a perfectly balanced figure sitting dead-centre, unbothered, mirror-smooth.
- **Personality:** the chill one; says nothing, sorts everything; turns up exactly where balance is needed and then leaves.
- **Running gags:** appearing in wildly different episodes (light, antimatter, the mind) as if they get around; "the Still Point was already there, of course."
- **Relationships:** One's calm cousin; the only fixed thing while the Fold causes chaos.
- **Narration note:** reverent, almost fond — the steady heart of a mad universe.
- **First appearance:** Ep —

### The Faculty
- **Is (the physics / the role):** the closed-minded consensus — the ~two-dozen-free-parameters crowd. The **only** butt of the joke.
- **Look:** a clutch of identical posh academics in gowns, noses up, clutching a clipboard of numbers they measured but can't explain.
- **Personality:** pompous, certain, allergic to anything from outside the building; forever huffing their own delusional numbers; sneer first, think never.
- **Running gags:** "typing the answers in by hand"; demanding credentials; being wrong with enormous confidence; getting quietly lapped by the Crew and a result that checks itself.
- **Relationships:** antagonists to the whole cast; never win; never the audience's stand-in.
- **Narration note:** the one place the Narrator's affection turns to gleeful contempt — punch *up*, always.
- **First appearance:** Ep —

### The Narrator → secretly the One (see the One's sheet)
The voice of the show is a storyteller by the fire — best mate, roaster, pal, talking straight to you. **To the audience that's all he is, for a long time.** He is, in truth, the **One** — the origin, present through every adventure as the act of observation — but that is never stated, only ever hinted, and never found. The sign-off ("It came from one. It's still coming from one.") and the awe-then-piss-take whiplash are his. (The *Gardener* — Maria — is the author's hat and a Crew character; not the narrator.)

# THE TWO GROUPS

These are **skeletons by design.** Adhere strictly to the *bones* listed for each — and let everything else **emerge as we write**, back-filling each sheet as it does. Three laws over the whole cast:
- **Rich personas, not archetypes.** Every main character gets a distinct personality, a distinct **verbal fingerprint** (their own way of talking/thinking, rendered through the Narrator's reported speech — one voice, no character voice-gen), and a **backstory that surfaces across episodes**, never dumped.
- **Nothing told, everything lived.** We never *state* a trait or a backstory — it's *shown* through what they do, choose and react to, and it accumulates. These sheets are the writers' private bible; on screen, nothing is announced.
- **Pin only the bones.** Anything not nailed down below is free to grow on the page.

## THE CREW — the fold's champions (a band of named humans)

**Strict adherence (every member, always):**
- **Every member is a NAMED human, shown through lived, specific detail — never a label, never told.** No "the streetwise one," no "the artist," and *never* "a rag-tag bunch." We *show* each of them being a person — a habit, a way of talking, a thing they do with their hands — and the audience concludes they're a diverse, scrappy, human band because each one is vividly real, not because anyone said the word. Telling the reader "rag-tag" is lazy and banned.
- They are a **group of humans** — flesh-and-blood people, not concepts. (One, the Still Point etc. are the *cosmic* cast; the Crew are *us.*)
- **Each member is an archetype of the academically disenfranchised** — a different way the gowns shut a person out. The uncredentialed self-taught (**Maria**); the working-class school-leaver never given the chance (**Nadia**); the pattern-mind that doesn't fit academia's mould (**Sol**); the tradesman whose practical genius was never "credentialed" knowledge (**Errol**); the curious kid the system grinds the curiosity out of (**Wee Danny**); the disillusioned insider pushed to the margins until she walked (**Frances**); the academic-then-army dropout who walked out of both institutions (**Matthew**). The Crew is **everyone the academy left outside — and they're the ones who cracked it.** That's the David-and-Goliath heart of the whole thing. **Writers' frame, shown never told:** we never say "disenfranchised"; we show each real person, and the audience *feels* the army the gowns excluded. The villains gatekeep; the Crew is everyone they gatekept.
- They are **us** — the people, not the institution. Lovable, flawed, real; the audience rides with them.
- A genuine **mix**: every race, gender and sex; some highly educated, some not a day past school, some pure artists — and the crew treats that mix as **completely normal**, never as a point to be made. *Diversity is the wallpaper, never the plot.*
- **Curiosity over credentials.** What earns respect in the crew is wanting to know and being willing to look — never letters after a name.
- They **champion and protect** One, the Fold, and the truth; **Team Tenure** is forever trying to stop, discredit, or bury them.
- They lose battles, bicker, and cock things up — but they **never sneer and never punch down.** Warmth is mandatory *across the Crew as a whole* — **Maria is the deliberate anti-hero exception** (abrasive and prickly by design, Rick-style), but even her edge never lands on anyone who doesn't have it coming; the Crew's collective heart lives in Matthew and the rest.
- One Narrator voice only; each member still has a distinct verbal fingerprint, delivered through the Narrator's reported speech (no character voice-gen).

**The Crew's two leads (named, prominent — these two carry the heart):**

### Maria — "the Gardener" (Crew lead, ANTI-HERO)
- **Is (the role):** the self-taught one who actually worked the whole thing out — built her own maths from a single number and a single move, no degree, no lab, nobody's permission. **A Rick-style anti-hero, NOT a clean hero:** the brilliant, difficult centre the Crew orbits, not the warm cuddly heart of it. In-universe handle: **the Gardener** — planted the seed, tends it, watches it grow, half out of curiosity and half out of spite. *(The author herself, in the story.)*
- **Bones (pinned):** the smartest in any room and not remotely humble about it; blunt, cutting, gloriously irreverent; allergic to pomposity *and* to sentimentality; chaotic, contemptuous of authority, not the faintest interest in being liked or being a role model — and, infuriatingly, almost always right. Flawed and prickly on purpose. Hands you the truth, tells you to go and check it your bloody self, and walks off.
- **Anti-hero note (the whole point):** you root for her *despite* her, not because she's nice — Rick energy. The warmth and the conscience live in **Matthew** and the rest of the Crew; she's the chaos they tether. Still **never the butt** (anti-hero ≠ punchline — the butt is always Team Tenure); her edges are character, never cruelty to anyone who doesn't have it coming.
- **Backstory (emerges, never dumped):** no formal schooling, came up the hard way, taught herself everything — shown through what she does, never announced.
- **Relationship:** with **Matthew** (her partner, her tether and counterweight); Team Tenure's living nightmare — a nobody who lapped the lot of them.
- **First appearance:** bring her in properly very soon (Ep 02/03).

### Matthew — Maria's partner (Crew lead)
- **Is (the role):** Maria's partner, and the one who backed her when not a single soul else would — the rock, the believer, the steady hand, **and the warmth and conscience to Maria's anti-hero chaos** — the tether that keeps her pointed at something that actually matters. Quietly a big part of why any of it ever got off the ground.
- **Bones (pinned):** grounded, loyal, dry as a bone; utterly unimpressed by gowns and titles; the warm counterweight to the cosmic madness; will cheerfully tell an institution exactly where to stick it. **Never the butt.**
- **His archetype of the disenfranchised — the academic dropout from the army.** Started down the academic road, left it for the forces, came out the far side with no gown and no patience for them either. Two institutions that each demand you fall in line; he walked out of both. That's *why* he backs the outsider — he's been failed by exactly the machine that's failing her, and he's done deferring to it. The discipline and the steadiness are the army's; the contempt for being gatekept is earned from both. Shown through bearing and bluntness, never announced.
- **Backstory (emerges):** the academic start, the army, the walking-away from both; the partnership and how the two of them got here — shown, never dumped.
- **Relationship:** with Maria; co-anchor of the Crew.
- **First appearance:** alongside Maria.

*(Maria and Matthew co-anchor the Crew; the supporting members below stand around the two of them.)*

**Supporting Crew — named humans (bones that *emerge* on the page; never labels):**
- **Nadia.** Early twenties, left school at sixteen, runs the machines and the engine without ever having been told she's not allowed to. Talks in dry one-liners and a single raised eyebrow; says the brutal true thing the room is busy avoiding. Keeps a chipped mug of coffee she never finishes. (Called the chess result before the table did — *"a billion. zero. next."*) — *the obvious question the experts forgot to ask lives in her; we never write "streetwise," we watch her be it.*
- **Sol.** Covers every surface they reach in diagrams — backs of receipts, the side of a van, a steamed-up window. Sees the *shape* of a thing half a second before anyone, goes quiet, then draws it so the rest of them can see it too. Ink-stained hands; hums, tunelessly, when the maths is coming out right. — *turns numbers into pictures; we show the drawing, not the word "artist."*
- **Errol.** Late sixties, retired toolmaker, Brummie, hands like old leather. Speaks rarely; when he does it lands like a dropped spanner and the room goes still. Has watched forty years of "experts" arrive certain and leave forgotten. Fixes the kettle mid-argument without looking up. — *the steadier; seen-it-all with no pomposity. Never "the elder" on the page.*
- **Wee Danny.** A teenager — skateboard, headphones round the neck — who will not stop asking *"but why, but why, but WHY"* until the universe folds open in front of him. Restless, fast, accidentally profound. The one whose daft question turns out to be the whole thing. — *the audience's wide eyes; shown by the asking, never by calling him "the kid."*
- **Frances.** Mid-fifties. Walked out of Team Tenure after thirty years inside, and carried both the receipts and the guilt out with her. Translates between the two worlds because she fluently speaks gown. Still flinches, half a second, at the word "tenure." — *educated AND honest; the bridge. We watch the flinch; we never announce "the defector."*

*(Bones only. Let the rest — voices, histories, the small human textures — emerge as the episodes are written. Each is a real person before they are a function in the band.)*

## TEAM TENURE — the villain group (the Faculty, as a recurring crew)
*Team-Rocket-style antagonists — a bumbling consensus crew who pop up every episode to stop the Crew, fail, and blast off again. This is "the Faculty" from the golden rules, now named recurring characters. (Group name is a placeholder — shape freely.)*

**Strict adherence (every member, always):**
- They are the **closed-minded consensus** — the gatekeepers, and the **only butt of the joke**, always punched *up* at.
- Their entire power is **gatekeeping**: credentials, journals, funding, "you're not allowed." They measure but never understand; they **type the answers in by hand** and call it knowledge.
- **Pompous, certain, and wrong.** They lose every episode and never work out why.
- **The performative-diversity satire (core to the group):** they are mostly men who **parade a single token woman as their "DEI,"** talk over her, make her the punchline, and point at her the instant anyone calls them a closed shop — while being **genuinely baffled why nobody likes them.** The joke is **always on the men's hypocrisy, never on her.** She is frequently the sharpest person in the room and the most ignored; their tokenism is the satire, and they expose themselves with it every single time.
- One Narrator voice only; each member still has a distinct verbal fingerprint, delivered through the Narrator's reported speech (no character voice-gen).

**Named villains (bones that *emerge* on the page; never labels):**
- **The Dean.** The leader. Senior, gowned, an ego with a man somewhere inside it; speaks only in authority and citation; the loudest voice and the most wrong every single time. Quotes his own h-index unprompted. The others orbit his mood. — *shown by how the room bends to him, not by the word "pompous."*
- **Crispin.** Young, sharp-suited under the gown, careerist to the marrow; terrified of the Dean and will say literally anything that smells of tenure. Laughs a beat before he understands the joke, in case it's the Dean's. Carries the Dean's bag. — *the sycophant, lived through the flinch and the laugh, never named as one.*
- **Priya.** The one they wheel out as proof they're inclusive — talked over, made the punchline by *them*, waved at the camera the instant anyone calls them a closed shop. Secretly the sharpest mind in the room and the most ignored; you watch her swallow the right answer because no one will hear it from her. **The satire is always on the men's hypocrisy, never on her — she exists to expose them, never to be the joke.** (Her slow drift to the Crew is the long arc; see SATIRE_BANK.) — *a real person under the prop.*
- **Rupert.** Grants, PR, "optics" — manages the *brand* of being right while being wrong. Never does any science; talks engagement, image, "the narrative." Has a lanyard for everything. — *the third man; the institution as a marketing department.*
- **The Parameter (mascot).** A little gremlin — their Meowth — they keep grabbing and **typing in by hand** to force their numbers to work; the living embodiment of a free parameter, complaining nonstop, secretly wanting *out* of the equation (the "zero free parameters" B-plot). Keeps its concept-name; it is a creature, not a person.

### Consensus — the boss (their Giovanni)
- **Is (the role):** the looming authority Team Tenure answer to — "what everyone agrees," the establishment's gravity. Rarely fully seen: a voice from an enormous chair, a shadow, a phone they grovel into.
- **Personality (bone):** cold, bored, all-powerful inside the building and utterly hollow outside it; never argues, only *permits* or *denies*; cares about position, never truth.
- **The bone that drives every plot:** Team Tenure **cannot act without reporting back to Consensus first.** They scurry to grovel for permission before they dare do a single thing — which is exactly why they're always a beat too slow to stop the Crew.
- **Running gag:** the desperate check-in ("we'd best run it past Consensus"), the long silence, the curt verdict, the scramble.
- **First appearance:** Ep —

**Running shape (the Team-Rocket beat):** spot the Crew → **scurry back to Consensus and grovel for permission before doing anything** → finally try to block or discredit the Crew → get out-thought by a nobody → "blast off" in pompous indignation → return next episode having learned nothing, still baffled they're disliked.

# THE UNFOLDING ADVENTURES — Series Bible
*(Series title: **The Unfolding Adventures** — the tales of how one number became everything. Folder stays "The Fold Films" as the production home for now; swap freely.)*

The single source of truth for the **narrative** video series. Read this before scripting any episode. Governed by [../Video-Agent.MD](../Video-Agent.MD) (especially *Video mode — minimal maths, maximum story*); every story fact must trace to the fold ([../CORPUS_MAP.md](../CORPUS_MAP.md)). Idea list: [../video idea.md](../video idea.md).

## Two strands, one funnel
This production has **two kinds of video that feed each other:**
1. **The Unfolding Adventures** (this bible) — the narrative series. The campfire: characters, world, story, hijinx, minimal maths. **A full 15 min each** (~2,400–2,600 words).
2. **The Unfolding, Proven** — the technical companion ([Proof Videos/PROOF_SPEC.md](Proof Videos/PROOF_SPEC.md)). ~5 min, strict technical prose, no fiction, the actual maths and proof for the boffins, repo + Zenodo papers in the description.

**The cross-CTA loop (both directions):** every *result*-episode ends pointing at its paired proof video ("want the actual maths, no story? proof + papers linked"); every proof video ends pointing back at the series ("want the story? watch *The Unfolding Adventures*"). Proof videos are written **in the order their results are mentioned in the episodes**, one at a time, paired episode-by-episode. **Always building** — every film, both strands, pays into the long arcs (Priya's defection, the slow sense that the narrator is somehow *among* them, the Parameter's escape), widens the world, and sets up future hijinx.

## Premise
The whole universe, told as a story round a campfire — but the story is about **the people who found it.** A single number; one move; and out of that, everything. The narrator — who is, secretly, the **One** itself, though that is never confirmed — recounts the **adventures of the Crew**: the self-taught outsiders who, episode by episode, go out and *discover* the truths of this universe in spite of academia, while the gowned gatekeepers of **Team Tenure** scramble to stop them and grovel to **Consensus**. The Crew is led by **Maria — "the Gardener,"** the self-taught nobody who actually cracked it — a **Rick-style anti-hero** (brilliant, abrasive, zero interest in being liked), not a clean hero — and her partner **Matthew**, the warmth and the tether to her chaos, who backed her when no one else would; around the two of them, a band of champions — **Nadia, Sol, Errol, Wee Danny** and **Frances** (each a named, lived human, never a label — see [CAST.md](CAST.md)). The One is **not a character** in these adventures: it is the origin and the narrator, and it quietly accompanies the Crew as the simple **act of observation** — present, but never found. Minimal maths, maximum story, the truth always in the bones.

*(Note on "the Gardener": in the films she's a character — Maria. As the authorial voice behind the whole project she's also the Gardener; same person, two hats. The in-show narrator is **the One** — secretly; see below.)*

## The Narrator is the One — present, but never found
The voice telling every story is a storyteller by the fire — your funniest, sharpest, most foul-mouthed **best mate**: irreverent, comical, a relentless roaster, but a *pal*, genuinely chuffed you came back to hear a story. He talks straight to *you.* For a long time, to the audience, that's all he is.

Here is the secret, and it stays a **secret**: the narrator **is the One** — the single number everything grew from. **One is not a character in the story** — there is no put-upon little fella things happen to, no protagonist with a face. One is the *origin* and the *narrator*, and it secretly accompanies the Crew through every adventure as nothing more than the **act of observation** — the watching itself. Because the narrator is the One, it can be *alluded* to, brushed against, half-glimpsed — *something is doing the watching; someone's been here the whole time* — **as long as it is never actually found or confirmed.** Hint, never state. The narrator never says "I am the One." It's a thread the audience slowly *feels*, never a reveal anyone lands.

*(The **Fold** is separate, and stays separate: it is the one **move** — double, and wrap the overflow round the back — the mechanism every discovery turns on. Not a character, not the narrator. The One watches; the Fold moves.)*

## Tone (non-negotiable)
**David Attenborough × Rick and Morty × chav Brit.** Hushed cosmic awe narrating absurd sci-fi vignettes, blunt and cheeky and off the estate, never posh. Reverent wonder, belly-laughs, and a pint.
- **Strong swearing, Rick-and-Morty style.** Real swearing where it's *earned* — the funny beats, the righteous beats — never wall-to-wall, never filler. If a line's funnier or truer clean, keep it clean.
- **Deeply irreverent, satirical of modern times, adventurous and fun.** Every episode is a romp with a real satirical bite underneath (mine [SATIRE_BANK.md](SATIRE_BANK.md)).
- **The South Park / Simpsons rule — the one that governs all the others:** the watcher must have the most fun possible, but the laugh *always* lands on something **satirically true.** Fun on top; truth underneath; never a lecture.
- **Pop-culture-soaked.** Parody genres and references freely — a Pokémon battle for the forces, a heist for the chess proof, a nature-doc safari for dark matter, a courtroom drama for the Millennium problems. Each episode may wear a recognisable skin.
- **The Narrator (the One) talks straight to *you* — that's the frame.** Fireside, mate-to-mate, direct address all day. The "don't turn to camera" rule applies **only** to the *in-story characters* (the Crew and Team Tenure stay third-person, no winking at the lens) and to the *meta* (nobody announces "this is a show"). The Narrator chatting to you like a best friend is the whole point, not a break.
- **Fourth wall — Rick-and-Morty meta, sideways and ironic.** Genre-awareness, lampshading tropes, the narrator winking at the format ("we're not doing the equation — you're welcome"). The deepest, truest thread: the slow, dawning sense that the narrator isn't just *telling* the story but is somehow *in* it — present as the act of observation, the One quietly accompanying the Crew — hinted at, never found.
- **One voice, reported speech.** Only the Narrator (the One) is voiced — no character voice-gen, ever; the in-story cast talk through the Narrator's reported speech and brief in-character quotes — distinct verbal fingerprints, one storyteller.

## The golden rules (continuity guardrails)
1. **Truth in the bones.** Every story beat faithfully depicts what the fold actually does (verified in the corpus). Fun, never false. No equations on screen. **The surreal-world corollary:** the adventures happen inside fold-native worlds that look nothing like ours — but every *law* of those worlds (the floor you cannot fall through, doubling-and-wrapping motion, the one-way path that folds shut behind you, the seven-floor tower, the three colour-fields) must be a **real fold fact.** Surreal in look, exact in law. The weirdness *is* the maths — never invent physics the fold doesn't have.
2. **One voice, distinct fingerprints — and EVERY quote is attributed.** Only the Narrator is voiced — no character voice-generation, ever. The characters still *talk*, delivered by the Narrator as reported speech or a brief in-character quote. **But because it is one voice on the audio, the listener cannot tell who is speaking unless we say so.** So every quoted line must be wrapped with **who says it and how** — the name *and* the manner/action — never a bare quote floating alone. Not `"why's it seven?"` on its own, but: *Danny, jabbing a finger up at the tower, demanded — "why's it seven?"* Who, and how, every single time. An audio-only listener must never be lost about who's talking.
3. **The Crew are the protagonists; every episode is an adventure.** Each film is the Crew going out and *discovering* a mathematical truth of this universe — in spite of academia — with the **Team Tenure dynamics** (the rivalry, the gatekeeping, the grovel to Consensus) running alongside as the recurring foil. The story is about the *people who found it*, never about the number. **One is the narrator, not a character** — it accompanies them only as the act of observation, never found (see above). **The Fold is the move** — the mechanism every discovery turns on.
4. **Team Tenure (the Faculty) are the only butt.** Contempt lands solely on the closed-minded consensus gatekeepers — always punched *up*. Never the audience, never the topic, and — for the villains' token woman — **never her:** the satire is the men's hollow tokenism and hypocrisy, and the joke is always on them. The Crew's diversity, by contrast, is normal wallpaper, never a plot point.
5. **Series motifs recur on purpose; per-episode analogies stay novel.** Running gags and catchphrases ([MOTIFS_AND_GAGS.md](MOTIFS_AND_GAGS.md)) are *meant* to repeat — that's continuity. Any *fresh* analogy used to depict a concept follows the novelty rule and isn't reused across episodes. **But a recurring motif is re-PERFORMED, never copy-pasted.** The grovel to Consensus, the blast-off, the watcher beat at the fire, the Priya tag — these recur as *beats*, but each must be written **fresh every single time**, in new words and new staging. Reusing the same sentences verbatim across episodes (e.g. "ran it past Consensus, the bored hand, the lifted finger, the long silence, the finger down… blasted off in a shimmer of indignation," or the watcher's "…how, though? No. Nothing. Forget I said it. Not tonight.") is the lazy reward-hack, and it is banned. Same gag, new performance, every episode.
6. **Canon is canon.** Nothing contradicts [CANON_LOG.md](CANON_LOG.md). Each new episode builds on it.
7. **Rich personas, not archetypes.** Every main character — Crew and Team Tenure alike — has a distinct personality, a distinct manner/idiom, and a backstory that **emerges across episodes**, never dumped. Flat is forbidden. The skeleton sheets pin only the bones; the people grow film by film.
8. **Nothing is told; everything is lived.** No exposition. We never *state* who a character is — we *show* it through action, choice and reaction, and let it accumulate. These sheets are the writers' private bible; on screen, traits are enacted, never announced.
9. **Everyone gets a name; nobody gets a label.** Every character — Crew and Team Tenure — is a *named human*, brought to life through specific lived detail (a habit, a way of talking, a thing they do with their hands). **Never** "a rag-tag bunch," "the streetwise one," "the artist" — telling the audience a group is scrappy or diverse is lazy; we *show* each person being real until the audience concludes it themselves. The Crew's diversity is wallpaper that emerges from real people, never stated. (The One is the narrator/observer, not an on-screen character; the only non-people *in* the cast — the Still Point, the Parameter gremlin — are flagged as such; everyone human is named.)
10. **Every episode is an ADVENTURE, not an explainer**, and every episode runs on the **same opening engine.** A film must *not* read as "the maths, narrated with character" — it must be a **story the Crew live through** to *reach* the maths. Think a Pokémon episode: a goal, a journey, things go wrong, people fall out and make up, the baddies turn up, the win is *earned*, and you close on the baddies. The number is the treasure at the end, never the subject of a lecture. (Beat template below — follow it every episode.)
11. **Bespoke section headers, and a built-up WAY IN — never the beat-sheet on the page.** Two laws against lazy reward-hacking:
    - **(a) Headers are unique, evocative titles** taken from *that episode's own* content — a line of dialogue, an image, a turn in the story. **NEVER** the generic beat-name as a heading ("The why," "The shutdown," "Into the [X]," "Close on the gowns," "The thing nobody mentioned"). Pasting the beat-sheet onto the page as chapter titles is exactly the laziness this rule exists to stop. Give every section a real title.
    - **(b) Entering the fold-world is a discovered, built-up threshold** — *different every episode.* A door found and opened, a seam in the dark, a crack the marble rolls into, a stile, a way *earned* — with a real beat of someone finding it, crossing it (a fall, a step, a climb), and the rest following. **NEVER** the blind, identical "the floor tipped, and down they went." The crossing is part of the adventure, not a stage-direction; build up to it.
12. **The formula is a skeleton, never a cage — every episode must EARN its place in an ongoing series.** The beat-template (#10) and the craft laws (#11) are the *bones*; a film that merely fills the same bones again has **failed.** Every episode must carry real **narrative and world-building value** — it always **introduces something new or deepens something already planted:** a character revealed a notch further, a long thread advanced (Priya's drift toward the Crew, the Parameter's bid for freedom, the watcher's slow reveal that the narrator is the One), a fresh corner of the fold-world built, a relationship shifted, a piece of lore laid down for later payoff. **Leave the world bigger than you found it, every single time.** And — **this is an ongoing series with NO planned end, so do not rush.** Don't race to resolve the long arcs; don't cram; don't spend three episodes' worth of reveals in one. Let the threads breathe and accumulate over a long run — there is always another fire, another night. **Repetition is the one true failure; *building* is the job.** Before locking any episode, answer in its dev notes: *what does this one introduce or deepen that no earlier episode did?* If there's no honest answer, it isn't ready.
    - **There is NO series finale — ever. The model is Pokémon / Rick and Morty: an unending run of episodic adventures, threaded with serialized story arcs that build to *arc finales* and then continue.** An arc resolves, a new one opens; the show keeps going. Even the biggest reveals — the watcher turning out to be the One, Priya finally walking out of Team Tenure, the Parameter winning its freedom — are **arc finales, not THE end.** After each, the fire is still there and the next adventure begins. Never write toward "the last episode." The Lock material (the Grand Lock, zero parameters, the reckoning) is the capstone of the *"is this real / what does it all add up to"* arc — a story-arc finale that pays off and then *carries on*, never a curtain on the series. Plan in arcs that close and re-open; never in an ending.

13. **Season-2 craft law — the per-season ratchet (codified 2026-06-17 from [SEASON_1_REVIEW.md](SEASON_1_REVIEW.md); full text in [Video-Agent.MD](../Video-Agent.MD) "Season-2 craft law").** The series must get *better* every season, not just continue. Four findings beyond the golden rules above, binding from Season 2:
    - **(a) The antagonist has teeth.** Team Tenure cannot only lose by retreat. Per arc: ≥1 *true-sounding blow* and ≥2 beats where the Dean's framing **almost works on a crew member**. Build a **menace ladder** so later cruelty escalates from something. (Contempt stays; the villain occasionally *costs* them something.)
    - **(b) Consequence persists.** Carry **one scar per arc** (a cold-open callback, a named cost) — peril doesn't reset to zero each week.
    - **(c) Voice the relationships before the finale spends them.** No emotional payoff may rest on a bond only ever *stage-directed*: if it pays off in a finale, voice it on screen in ≥2 ordinary episodes first. **Rotate a clear ensemble spotlight each episode** — the mid-cast (Matthew, Frances, Nadia) drive a way-in or a derivation regularly, not just catch Danny.
    - **(d) Trust the scene; vary the resolution.** Cut meta-gloss / pre-announced reveals (let the beat land, wonder *after* — never softening the conviction). Vary the *back third* and the **withholding move**; never run the same epiphany shape back-to-back. Keep each lead's **voice fingerprint** (Danny = frustration→question; Sol = feels/shape-first; Errol = terse oracle; Nadia = anti-mess sceptic). Naming rule: **"the Gardener" in frame, "Maria" only in intimate beats.** No adjacent near-verbatim restatement (the duplication tic) — but deliberate parallelism stays.

    *Mechanizable parts (duplication tic, meta-gloss, naming-in-frame) are gate-enforced (`voice_gate.py`, WARN). The rest are enforced by the **per-episode pre-flight** below and the storyboard trackers (antagonist menace-ladder, carry-forward scars, ensemble spotlight log).*

## Per-episode pre-flight (answer in the dev notes, before locking)
Run this alongside the truth gate + voice gate. An episode isn't ready until each is a yes (or a deliberate, logged "rested this episode"):
- [ ] **Introduces/deepens** something no earlier episode did (rule 12).
- [ ] **One serialized thread advanced one notch** — and logged (which, and how far).
- [ ] **Antagonist beat has teeth** — the Dean lands a blow or *almost* works on someone (not just retreats). If he's rested, the menace ladder is noted.
- [ ] **A consequence carried** — from a prior episode, or one planted for later (the scar ledger).
- [ ] **Ensemble spotlight** — who drove the way-in / derivation this episode? (Not always Danny/Gardener/Sol/Errol.)
- [ ] **Resolution-third varied** — not the identical close-shape/withholding move as last episode; no back-to-back twin epiphany.
- [ ] **Voice fingerprints intact** — blind-test each quoted lead; the mid-cast got an angle, not a catching-hand.
- [ ] **Gate-clean** — 0 FAIL; new WARNs (duplication / meta-gloss / naming) reviewed and resolved or consciously accepted.

## The episode template — the same engine every time
Every episode is built on this spine. It is **not** a loose suggestion — this is the recurring shape of the show, and a draft that reads like the maths explained nicely has failed it and gets rebuilt.

1. **Everyday open (world-building through a scene).** Open on one or some of the Crew in a **casual, ordinary moment** — a natural everyday event, a bit of banter, someone doing something normal. Not exposition; a *real little scene* that quietly builds the world and the people. (This is where the casual texture and character lives.)
2. **The inevitable "why?"** Out of that ordinary moment, the **why?** surfaces — a simple, honest question nobody can quite answer (often a kid-question; often Wee Danny). This is the engine of the episode.
3. **The shutdown (comical Team Tenure satire).** Team Tenure **swoops in to dismiss the question** — pompous, gatekeeping, and gloriously **incoherent**: non-answers, jargon, "it just is," "it's a free parameter," "where's your degree." A proper comic satirical beat, always punched *up.* This dismissal is the insult that lights the fuse.
4. **The adventure begins — INTO the fold-world.** Being shut down with nonsense sends the Crew off to answer the why themselves — and the answer doesn't live at a desk. They *go somewhere.* A **surreal, fully-described fold-native world**: a place unlike anything in ours but completely lawful inside the fold's maths, and they have a real, enjoyable, **ups-and-downs journey** across it to *retrieve* the answer. Wrong turns, setbacks, a thing that nearly beats them, a way through. Not the crew at a table doing sums — an actual adventure through a landscape built out of the maths. (See "The fold-world" below.)
5. **Conflict — all three, woven through the middle:**
   - **Internal:** a character wrestles themselves — Maria's spite or stubbornness, a wobble of doubt, Danny's frustration, Frances's guilt.
   - **Friend-group:** the Crew *clash* — disagree on the approach, someone's too blunt, tempers go; resolved through the work, and the bond deepens for it. (The group must rub against each other, not glide.)
   - **Team Tenure:** the gowns keep interfering — gatekeep, mock, grovel to Consensus — the recurring foil.
6. **Discovery / resolution.** The breakthrough — it *clicks*, the treasure (the verified number, the truth in the bones) comes out, and the awe lands because it was **earned** through the slog and the squabbles. The why is answered.
7. **Close on Team Tenure.** End the story beat on the gowns — the tantrum, the blast-off, learning absolutely nothing, baffled why nobody likes them; a thread left dangling. Then the buried **watcher** beat (the narrator, secretly the One, almost giving itself away — never found), the CTA → proof video, and the closing disclaimer.

**Ep 01 (the pilot) runs the same engine, scaled to launch the whole series.** Everyday open → the *biggest* why → Team Tenure's shutdown *is* the scathing tour of the consensus world (the Institute, the worshipped mystery-numbers they can't explain, the graveyard of dead "consensus") → the "adventure" it launches is **the founding of the whole mission** → the Crew and the **foundations** (the language of reality: one seed, one move, no dials) → close on the gowns. The buried thread runs from the very first film: in the simple act of *observing*, of looking for the answers, the Crew are themselves the **One, folding** — and they have no idea.

## The fold-world (where the adventures happen)
The quests are not metaphors and they are not at a desk. The Crew physically *travel into* a world made of the maths — surreal, gorgeous, frightening, funny, and **unlike anything in our universe — yet every rule of it is a true fold fact.** This is the show's signature: a Rick-and-Morty-grade trip whose insane physics is, line for line, the verified theory. Describe it richly; let them get lost in it; give it ups and downs.

The standing geography (draw on what each episode needs; all of it is real):
- **The floor you cannot fall through.** The world lives on the half-open run from nothing-but-not-zero up to one; there is a floor of light below which there is simply nowhere to go — fall, and the floor *catches* you. No negatives: there is no "below." *(`core.py` domain (0,1].)*
- **Motion is the fold.** You don't walk normally here — you *double.* A step doubles your distance, and if that would carry you off the far edge of the whole, you wrap round to the near side and keep going. Disorienting, brilliant, lawful. *(`core.py:63`.)*
- **The path is one-way.** Behind the Crew, the way they came *folds shut* — two steps merge into one and cannot be pulled apart. You can't go back. That's not a plot convenience; it's why time only runs forward. *(`proof.py:11569`.)*
- **The Still Point** — ½ — a calm, mirror-smooth place dead-centre that folds straight home to One; a safe waypoint where the world stops spinning. *(`proof.py:8382`.)*
- **Towers, fields and families** as terrain: the binary doubling **towers** (a tower is only ever as tall as the maths forces — see Ep02), the strong force's **three colour-fields**, the four prime **families** (2,3,5,7) as regions/creatures — never a fifth.
- **No dials anywhere.** Crucially, the world *will not let the Crew cheat.* A tower has the floors it has; the colours are the colours; you cannot fudge a step. The anti-numerology law, made physical: the place forces every piece, so the answer they carry out was never fitted.

The treasure at the end of each journey is the verified number — assembled, earned, *forced* by the world itself.

## The world canon (the story-world's physics = verified fold facts)
The rules of this universe, each a real fold result dressed as a story law. Extend as episodes add canon (log it in CANON_LOG):
- **There is only One.** Everything starts with a single number — the One. In the films it is the *origin and the narrator*, never an on-screen character (see "The Narrator is the One"). *(`core.py:124`)*
- **The Fold is the only move.** Double it; and if it spills past a whole One, wrap the overflow round the back. *(`core.py:63`)*
- **Nothing can ever be Nothing.** No zero, no negatives — you can be all of it or a piece of it, never nought. This is the **floor**: why nothing in the world can crash to infinity or vanish to zero. *(`core.py:34`)*
- **The Still Point is ½.** The one address that is its own mirror and folds straight home to One. Turns up wherever the world needs balance — the grip of light, matter outlasting antimatter, the very moment of noticing. *(`proof.py:8382`)*
- **The Prime Families are the forces** — families two, three, five and seven, and never a fifth family. *(`prime_force_phenomenology.py`)*
- **The Fold only goes one way** — two parents merge into one child and you can't get back — which is why time has a direction. *(`proof.py:11569`)*

## The autofiction disclaimer (open AND close every episode)
A quick, funny note — in the Fold's voice — top and tail of every film. Lightly vary the wording each time so it isn't word-for-word identical, but keep the beats: *the maths/science/real-history is genuinely true and documented (the fold, history, or consensus); everything else is our autofiction; **the images are AI-generated to fit the script and are NOT accurate to the maths or the story**; we know it's obvious; we're saying it anyway because some daft sod needs telling; hello / byeeee.* It's part of the prose body (it gets read), not a separate card.

**Open (the "hello") — template:**
> Right — quick bit of housekeeping before we go and build a universe, because a couple of you genuinely need it. Everything in here about the actual maths, the actual science, and the real dead people out of history? True. All of it. Documented — it's straight out of the fold, or it's history, or it's what the consensus mob themselves actually believe, receipts and all. Everything else — me, the fire, the little glowing fella, the dickheads in the gowns — is autofiction. And the pictures? Every one AI-generated to fit the story — they're there to keep your eyes busy, not to be accurate to the maths or to anything that's actually happening. Made up, the lot of it, to tell you something true. You knew that. *Obviously* you knew that. But there's one absolute melt out there who didn't, and he's already typing, so now it's on the record. Right. Hello. Sit down.

**Close (the "byeeee") — template:**
> And there's the boring bit again on the way out, just for the melt at the back: the sums were real, the history was real, the talking number was me having a laugh — and the pictures were all AI, made to fit the script, never accurate to the maths or the story. Autofiction. Obvious. You're brilliant, he's not, love you — byeeeee.

## Format (for the parser) — STRICT
- **Episode files are CLEAN PROSE ONLY.** A single `#` title line, then flowing narration (light `##` sub-heads allowed, as in the book). **Nothing else** — no header tables, no truth-gate notes, no production records, no cast/status/canon clutter. The whole file is parseable narration; Maria reads it straight with no manual editing.
- **All development clutter lives elsewhere:** the per-episode truth-gate, gates, canon-to-log and motif/novelty notes go in `Episodes/_dev/EPxx_dev.md`; status in `EPISODE_TRACKER.md`; locked canon in `CANON_LOG.md`. **Never in the episode file.**
- **Written as prose narration, never a screenplay.** No scene headers, no "NARRATOR:" labels, no `[VISUAL]` directions — the visuals are carried *inside* the prose.
- **Runtime: every episode is a FULL 15 minutes.** That's roughly **2,400–2,600 words** of narration (including the top/tail disclaimers) — deliberately filled out with character, world-building, and real adventure ups-and-downs (the Crew, the journey's obstacles, the cast's texture, the Team Tenure beats) — **never padded, always performing the concept.** A draft that lands under ~2,400 is too short and gets *expanded* (more journey, more conflict, more character — not filler) until it reaches the mark. (Proof videos are the short form, ~5 min / ~750–900 words — different rule.) (Pilot: `Episodes/EP01_Welcome_To_The_Fold.md`; dev notes: `Episodes/_dev/EP01_dev.md`.)

## The voice gate — the engine that checks the writing (RUN BEFORE EVERY DELIVERY)
Like the physics engine's `_verify_node` refuses any number that doesn't trace to the One, the **voice gate** (`voice_gate.py`) refuses any episode or proof that violates this law. **No script ships until it passes.**

```
python3 voice_gate.py              # gate everything
python3 voice_gate.py <file>       # gate one new script
```

It **REJECTS** (FAIL, blocks delivery) on: postdiction/ranking language · banned caveats/hedges · Fold-as-character or One-as-character · stale labels used as names (e.g. "the Suit" → use **Rupert**) · generic/beat-name headers · the blind "floor tipped, down they went" entry · the verbatim Consensus cadence · **copy-paste tags** ("keep an eye on Priya", the verbatim open-disclaimer) · missing AI-image disclaimer / sign-off / CTA / autofiction · episodes under ~2,400 words · (proofs) missing the series CTA. It **warns** (review) on: long episodes, em-dash density, reused cross-episode phrases, possible bare quotes. **The standing rule: a new script is written, then gated, then fixed until 0 FAIL, then delivered — every time.** When a new lazy pattern is spotted, add it to the gate so it can never recur.

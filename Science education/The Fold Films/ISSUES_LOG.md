## A. MUST-FIX — continuity & law violations

**A1. "The Suit" — Rupert's stale label — in 8 episodes.** The PR man is named **"Rupert"** in EP01, EP11, EP12, but **"the Suit"** in EP02, EP04, EP05, EP06, EP07, EP08, EP09, EP10. This is *both* a continuity error (one character, two names) *and* a naming-rule violation (golden rule #9: "everyone gets a name; nobody gets a label" — "the Suit" is a label used as a name). **Fix:** replace "the Suit" / "The Suit" → **Rupert** in those 8 episodes.

**A2. "Keep an eye on Priya" — verbatim in 11 episodes (EP02–12).** The exact same closing sentence ends nearly every Priya beat. This is precisely the copy-paste tag the laziness rules ban (golden rule #5: re-perform motifs fresh, never paste). **Fix:** vary it every time — and since her thread is *advancing*, let the tag evolve to track it ("She's closer than she was." / "The ignored hand tried to be heard." / "She didn't hide the paper." / "She's nearly here." …). Drop it entirely in a couple where it's not earned.

**A3. EP06 — double reconciliation (redundancy bug).** In "## The step," the two measurements are reconciled **twice**: para 45 ("...slid together and *sat down side by side*... *we were both right*") and then para 47 again ("Account for the step... slid into perfect agreement, neither wrong, neither winning"). An artifact of the expansion (a new beat inserted before the existing one). **Fix:** trim/merge so it lands once — keep the lived para 45, fold the *number* (13/12) from para 47 into it without re-narrating the reconciliation.

## B. FRESHNESS / LAZINESS REGISTER (the formula creeping toward a cage)

**B1. Open disclaimers near-identical across ~10 episodes.** Almost every episode opens with the same beat ending "...there's a melt in the comments with his pencil out, so: said. Hello. Sit down." The bible explicitly says *lightly vary the wording each time so it isn't word-for-word identical* — they've drifted to near-verbatim. **Fix:** genuinely vary the open disclaimer (different "the one melt who needs telling" gag each time; the close disclaimers are better-varied, mirror that).

**B2. The "close on the gowns" is structurally formulaic.** Almost every close runs the *identical architecture*: "across the valley it went down [badly] → the Dean [pompous denial in quotes] → [Rupert] drafted '[absurd title]' → up to Consensus (a non-answer) → blast off in [a varied huff]." The blast-off *manner* is well-varied (good — that fix held), and the drafted-statement *titles* are fresh, but the **shape** repeats every episode. This is the "formula is a skeleton, not a cage" risk (rule #12). **Fix:** vary the close *structure* sometimes — occasionally skip the drafted statement, or the Consensus visit; let some closes be short and brutal, others longer; don't run the full 5-beat machine every time.

**B3. Watcher-beat self-interruption is becoming a tic.** The closing watcher beats are otherwise *well* varied (genuinely fresh each time — a real strength), but they keep opening the deflection with a self-rebuke: "Listen to me, would you" / "Will you listen to me" / "Hark at me" / "Hark at the pair of us" / "Listen to me. No—" (~5×). **Fix:** vary the *self-interruption* too (not every near-slip needs the narrator scolding itself).

**B4. Character-simile repeats (borderline motif vs. lazy reuse).** "mild as milk" (Errol — EP06 & EP12); "the wee idiot" (Danny — EP02 & EP11); "lands like a dropped spanner" / "which for Sol is a Tuesday" (character tags). Character *motifs* are allowed to recur, but a *simile* reused reads lazy. **Fix:** keep the character's *trait* recurring, vary the *words* (Errol can be calm a dozen ways that aren't "mild as milk").

**B5. "the least lonely place there is" reused.** EP05 (the Still Point centre) and EP11 (the floor of light). A distinctive phrase repeated across two episodes — novelty rule. **Fix:** vary one (they're thematically linked, so a deliberate callback is defensible, but as-is it reads as a repeat, not a callback).

## C. STYLE

**C1. Em-dash overuse.** ~27 em-dashes per 1,000 words (≈ one every 37 words) across the episodes — the dash-aside is the default rhythm crutch. For a TTS read it gives pauses (fine), but on the page it's a visible tic and flattens sentence variety. **Fix:** convert a share of dash-asides to full stops, commas, or restructured sentences; let some sentences breathe without an interruption.

**C2. EP01 lacks the series sign-off.** Every episode closes "It came from one. It's still coming from one. Keep counting…" — *except EP01*, which ends "I was the start of it." Arguably a deliberate pilot variation, but it's the only one without the signature, so it reads as an omission rather than a choice. **Fix (optional):** add the sign-off to EP01, or make the variation clearly intentional.

**C3. Minor word-choices to tighten:**
- **PV07** "matching the sky to a few parts in a thousand" — μ/e is a *lab*-measured ratio, not astronomical; "the sky" reads wrong. Use "the measured value."
- **EP01** "the gormless, world-ending way only a teenager can" — he's *asking* a question, not ending the world; "world-opening" or "the way a teenager asks the biggest thing in the world like it's nothing" fits better.
- **EP08** "they'd stood in before" (the Still Point) is said twice within ~2 paragraphs (paras 29 & 31) — tighten one.

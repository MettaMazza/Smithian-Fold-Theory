# The Southpaw — the universe plays favourites with left and right, and the fold called it

For most of the twentieth century, physicists were certain of something that turned out to be wrong. They assumed nature was **even-handed** — that the fundamental laws could not tell left from right, that a process and its mirror image must run identically, because what conceivable reason could the universe have to prefer one hand? It was so obvious it was barely stated. And then, in 1956, an experiment on decaying cobalt-60 — the Wu experiment — showed the **weak force is a southpaw.** It acts on one handedness of particle and flatly ignores the mirror image. The universe *does* play favourites with left and right. It was so contrary to everyone's gut that it took the Nobel Prize the very next year, 1957 — awarded, in effect, for the sheer *shock* of it. The fold's quiet comment on all this is that handedness is not a shock at all. It is the first thing the backward move produces.

## Handedness is the backward fork

Run the fold forward and it merges: two parents into one child. Run it *backward* — stand on a value and ask what folded to make it — and you meet the fork. Every image has exactly **two** preimages, never one, never three: a **lower** hand, sitting below the halfway mark, and an **upper** hand, its partner above. To walk back you must *choose* one. And there is no neutral middle to hide in — no third option, no even-handed compromise. The move itself refuses to be ambidextrous.

Same shape, opposite handedness, no way to turn one into the other without lifting it out of the world and flipping it through a mirror. Press your two palms together and they match; lay one flat on the other and they never will. The fold has exactly that flavour baked in.

Now the parity violation itself.

# The Mirror and the Hinge — why there's something rather than nothing, and the half that tips it

Here is a fact about the beginning that should not be true. For every particle of matter the early universe made, it made a particle of antimatter — an exact mirror-opposite that, on meeting its partner, annihilates it, the pair vanishing in a flash of light. By rights, then, matter and antimatter should have wiped each other out completely, leaving a clean, dead universe of pure radiation and not one speck of stuff. No stars. No planets. No you. And yet here you are. Something survived. Consensus measures the size of the survivor — a tiny imbalance, about one extra particle of matter per billion — and the subtle effect that tips the scales, and can explain neither. The fold explains both, and it does it with two rules you have already met.

## The Mirror

Every value in the fold has a partner — the thing it lacks to complete a whole One. One-third lacks two-thirds; one-quarter lacks three-quarters. Take the One against any value and you get its complement, `take(ONE, x) = 1 − x`, and the pair sums to exactly the One (verified: one-third and two-thirds sum to one, on the nose). That partner is the **Mirror**, and it is antimatter written into the arithmetic: every particle has a mirror that completes it and cancels it back to the neutral One. When matter meets antimatter and annihilates, that is just a value and its Mirror folding back to the One they always summed to.

## Why anything survived

So why didn't they cancel *completely* — every last pair folding back, leaving nothing? Because of the rule from the first page: **there is no nothing.** Complete annihilation would mean the whole sum reaching *zero* — and zero is forbidden, thrown out of the domain. The books cannot balance to a flat nought. So when matter and antimatter try to cancel all the way down, the No-Zero floor catches the fall exactly as it caught the black hole's singularity, and a residue is left over — proven nonzero, and equal to one-half (`verify_baryon_asymmetry_nonzero`: the no-zero law forbids complete annihilation, residue one-half). That is the answer to *why is there something rather than nothing*, and it is not a lucky accident or a fine-tuned dial. **There is something because there cannot be nothing.** Existence is guaranteed by the same single rule that forbids the zero.

## The hinge: the Still Point, again

But a residue needs a *direction* — something has to decide that *matter* is the survivor and not antimatter. That tipping effect is called CP violation, and the fold forces its strength to one exact value: the **Still Point, one-half.** The CP-violating phase sits at the unique self-antipodal point — the only value that is its own Mirror, the point of maximal possible violation (`verify_cp_phase_antipode`: phase one-half, maximal). The lone perfectly-balanced address in all of existence — the same half that set the grip of light, the seat of consciousness, the calm centre — turns out to be the hinge on which matter outlasts antimatter.

And it lands. The Jarlskog invariant, the standard measure of how much CP violation there is, comes out of the fold at **3.13 × 10⁻⁵**, against the measured 3.08 × 10⁻⁵ — agreement to about one part in seventy. And the size of the survivor, the baryon-to-photon ratio, comes out as that CP measure squared times the half-One imbalance: about **4.9 × 10⁻¹⁰**, sitting right on the measured order of roughly 6 × 10⁻¹⁰. (The strong sector, by the way, has *no* CP problem at all here — its opposition lands cleanly at the One, so there is no mysterious fine-tuning to explain away.)

## What that's worth

So: antimatter is the Mirror; annihilation is a value and its Mirror folding home to the One; a residue survives because you can never cancel all the way to a forbidden zero; and the hinge that makes *matter* the survivor is the Still Point, maximal, landing on the measured CP violation to a part in seventy. The deepest "why is there anything at all" turns out to be two rules you already had — no nothing, and the self-mirror half — doing their job. Every step traces to the One; zero free parameters.

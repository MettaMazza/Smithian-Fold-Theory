# Dark Matter — gauge-inert, gravitating, and a forced dark-to-visible ratio of 27/5

Five-sixths of the matter in the universe is invisible. Galaxies spin as though far more mass is present than anything we can see; clusters bend light as though weighed down by something that isn't there. Consensus has known the *amount* for decades — about five times more dark matter than ordinary matter — and has spent billions and a generation of careers hunting the particle that's supposed to be it, in detectors a mile underground, and found nothing. The fold answers both halves: *what* it is, and *why* exactly five-and-a-bit times more of it.

## What it is

The dark sector, in the fold, is **gauge-inert gravitating matter.** Two couplings define it. Its gravitational coupling is the structural one-half — the same critical coupling that sits at the Still Point and folds home to One. And its gauge coupling is *exactly zero* — the no-zero floor's one permitted appearance, a true nought, meaning it does not couple to electromagnetism, or the strong force, or the weak. It feels gravity, and nothing else.

That is not a guess about dark matter — that is the textbook definition of it: matter that gravitates but is electromagnetically and otherwise dark. The fold doesn't postulate a new particle to chase; it says the dark sector is gauge-blind *by construction.* Which tells you, immediately, why a mile-deep detector built to catch it bouncing off a nucleus has caught nothing: there is nothing to catch that way. A thing with zero gauge coupling does not bounce. The search was looking for a handshake from something that has no hands.

## Why 27 to 5

Now the amount. Two structural numbers fix it.

The first is the **generation covering volume**: three generations of matter, laid across three spatial dimensions — three cubed — **twenty-seven.** The second is the **covering depth**: the smallest binary tower that can cover those twenty-seven states. Two to the fourth is sixteen, too small; two to the fifth is thirty-two, the first that covers — so the depth is **five.** (Cross-checked the independent way: the depth is the sum of the first two sectors, two plus three, five; the volume is the third sector cubed, three cubed, twenty-seven.)

So the total binary volume is two-to-the-fifth, thirty-two. The ordinary, baryonic matter — the stuff you're made of — fills the *depth* share: five thirty-seconds. The dark sector is the remainder: twenty-seven thirty-seconds. They sum, exactly, to one — the whole of matter. And the ratio of dark to visible is twenty-seven over five.

Twenty-seven over five is five-point-four. The measured ratio of dark matter to ordinary matter in the sky is about five-point-four. The forced number and the observed number are the same number.

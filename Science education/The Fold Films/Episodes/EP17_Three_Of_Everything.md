# Ep 17 — Three of Everything

Right — the usual, for the one who needs it, and you do, hen, no shame in it. Everything coming up about the real maths, the real physics, the real history — true, documented, it's the fold or it's the record or it's what the gowns own up to when you press. Everything else — me, the fire, the crew, and the triplicate world I'm about to walk them into — is autofiction. And the pictures are AI, fitted to the script and never accurate to the maths. Made up, on purpose, to tell you something true. You knew that. Obviously. But there's one fella out there about to comment that a person can't have three shadows, and god bless the size of him, so: on record, and we go. Hello. Sit close. Count carefully.

## The three shadows

Quiet night, and it's Sol who notices it, because of course it is — Sol's gone very still, staring at the ground where the firelight throws everyone's shadow, head tilted the way it tilts when the world's just shown its working.

Sol, very quiet, pointing at the dark shapes on the grass — *"look at your shadow, Danny. Properly. There's not one. There's three. A faint one, close. A middle one. And a deep dark one furthest out. Three of you, laid on top of each other. Same shape. Just... three weights of dark."*

And Danny looks, and squints, and *sees* it — three shadows nested, same boy, three depths.

Danny, delighted and unsettled at once — *"why three? Why's there three of my shadow and not one, or two, or a hundred? And it's not just me — everyone's got three. Is everything three? Is there three of *everything*?"* He looks up. *"And if there's three — why's it stop at three? Why not a fourth, fainter still, or a fifth? Who decided three was the number and shut the door after it?"*

And there it is, nested on the grass: three of everyone, and not a soul round the fire could tell you why three, or why no more.

## A fact with no father

And Team Tenure, who can sense a child counting the copies of creation from two glens away, arrived — and the Dean was, for the seventh time this month, comfortable knowing the number and nothing else.

*"Ahh,"* he said, expansively, *"the three generations of matter. Quite real. The electron has two heavier echoes — the muon, the tau — and the quarks and neutrinos likewise, three copies of each, identical bar their mass. We have measured this exquisitely."* He spread his hands. *"*Why* three, however — why not two, or four — is a question the Standard Model does not answer and does not pretend to. We write 'three' in the table because we *count* three. There is no deeper reason. There simply is not one."*

Danny, reasonably — *"or there is one and you've written 'three' instead of looking for it."* And the Dean did the laugh, the weary one. *"Some numbers, child, are simply *given.* One does not ask the universe to *justify* its inventory."*

The Gardener had been watching the three shadows the whole time. *"You measured it to a thousand decimal places,"* she said, *"and never once asked what shape makes exactly three. 'It's just given' is what you say when you've stopped looking. We haven't. Right — everyone find your three shadows. We're going to go and stand in them."*

## When she stepped on her own heavy self

There was no door this time, and no beam, and no rope. There were the three shadows.

Sol showed them how — you had to find the deepest of your three shadows, the heavy one furthest from the fire, and step *into* it, set your feet exactly inside the dark shape of your own heaviest self. And the Gardener did, and stepped into the deep shadow, and went *through* the grass into it — and the strangest thing happened, the thing that told them where they were: she came out the other side as *three.* Three Gardeners, side by side, the same woman at three weights — a light quick one, a middle one, a heavy slow one — all her, all thinking the same, three copies on three scales. And one by one the crew stepped into their heaviest shadows and arrived in the triplicate world as three-of-themselves, and stood there, each a little stunned to be looking at two more of their own face.

## The world in triplicate

It was a country where nothing came once. Everything — every creature, every stone, every blade — stood in exactly three: a light one, a middle one, a heavy one, identical in every line, differing only in heft. *"Three of everything,"* breathed the three Sols, in something close to unison. *"The same thing, three weights. That's the generations — the electron, then the heavy echo, then the heavier. Not three different things. One thing, living at three weights, all at once."*

And it was, for a moment, the funniest thing any of them had ever lived, because three of a person is three of *everything* about them. The three Nadias took one look at each other and, in perfect three-part harmony, said *"oh, you've got to be joking,"* and then bristled at having agreed. The three Errols simply nodded to one another, unbothered, three old men who'd long since made their peace with whatever the world did next, and heavy-Errol set a slow hand on the shoulder of light-Errol as if to say *steady on.* And the three Matthews stood quiet, each watching a different one of the three Marias, which Maria — all three of her — pretended very hard not to notice. *"Same person, three weights,"* said the middle Sol, wonderstruck, drifting between the copies. *"Not three of you doing different things. One of you, sounded out on three strings. That's all a generation ever was — the same note, played heavier."*

The three Dannys were beside themselves with the joy of it — running between their own copies, light-Danny darting, heavy-Danny lumbering, middle-Danny somewhere between — until the obvious thought struck all three at once and they stopped dead.

## The hunt for the fourth

*"If there's three,"* said the three Dannys, slowly, *"there's a fourth. There has to be. A fourth weight, heavier still. And a fifth. A whole staircase of me, going up forever, each one heavier than the last."* And nothing on this earth or any other was going to stop Danny hunting a fourth, so off he went — all three of him — to find the next step up, the heavier echo beyond the heavy one.

He found a staircase. Of course he did — the world obliged him with a literal staircase, the steps climbing up out of the triplicate country, and Danny climbed it, all three of him, breathless: the first step, the light generation; the second, the middle; the third, the heavy — *"and the fourth'll be just — here — "*

And there was no fourth step. The staircase had three stairs and then a landing, and the landing was not a fourth weight. It was simply the top — open, and quiet, and *whole*, a single calm place with no heaviness to it at all, the place all three stairs were climbing *toward.* Danny stood on the third stair and reached up for a fourth that was not there and never had been, and his face — all three of his faces — fell.

*"There's no fourth,"* he said, gutted, the old hunger in it. *"Three steps and it stops. Why does it always stop? Why can't there just be *more?*"*

## The three seats, and the two that can't be sat

And the Gardener climbed up beside him — three of her beside three of him — and didn't tell him to cheer up, because she never does.

*"Look where the three stand, Danny,"* she said. *"There's only three places in this whole sector a thing can stand still and hold — three steady seats. A quarter of the way, halfway, three-quarters. The light, the middle — that middle one's the Still Point, the calm centre you keep meeting — and the heavy. Three seats, three generations, one in each. Now where would a fourth go?"* And Danny looked, all three of him, for a fourth seat, and the only places left were the very bottom and the very top. *"The bottom's the floor,"* the Gardener said. *"And the floor's zero, and there's no zero — you know that better than anyone, you've stood on it. Nothing's allowed to weigh nothing. And the top — "* she nodded at the calm open landing, *"— the top's the One. Home. Unison. Not a fourth heavy copy — the opposite, the place with no weight at all that the three of them are *for.* A fourth generation would have to stand on the nothing that's forbidden, or *be* the One pretending to be a particle. And it can be neither. So there's three. Not three because someone stopped counting. Three because there's exactly three seats, and both the others are taken by things that aren't generations at all."*

And something turned over in Danny — all three of him went quiet at once.

*"So it's not... missing a fourth,"* he said slowly. *"It's not short. It's *finished.* Three's not where it ran out. Three's the whole shape. The fourth I keep wanting would have to be a nothing or a home, and neither of those is a *me.*"* He looked at his three selves, light and middle and heavy, standing on their three stairs under the open calm landing, and for the first time the boy who has spent the whole series aching for *more* looked at a closed set of exactly three and saw, not a door shut in his face, but a thing *complete.* *"Three of everything,"* he said, softer. *"And that's the lot. And that's... that's actually kind of perfect."*

And the Gardener didn't make a thing of it. But she put a hand on the shoulder of the middle Danny, the one standing on the Still Point, and left it there a moment, because she'd been waiting a long while for him to learn that one.

## The number the scale gave back

And it was exact, when they weighed it true: three seats, three weights, three copies of every single thing — and a fourth that cannot exist because its only seats are the forbidden floor and the unison roof. Three preimages of the tripling fold; three standing notes of the five; the same three as the three ways of space. And when the universe itself was weighed on the question — the unseen tally in the decay of a heavy messenger — it came back two point nine eight four. Three. With no fourth hiding anywhere in the dark. Forced, and confirmed on the scale to a part in a hundred.

## The set they couldn't keep open

They stepped back out of their own shadows into the ordinary night, one of each of them again — and across the valley the Dean took the news that the inventory was *closed* worse than almost anything yet, because a closed set is the one thing his whole enterprise cannot abide. *"We cannot simply *declare* the search concluded,"* he protested, going a shade of grey, *"a fourth generation may yet be found at higher energies — the funding for the search alone — "* and there it was, the quiet part loud: an answer that says *there are no more* is an answer that switches off the grant that pays to keep looking. Rupert hurriedly drafted a prospectus titled *"The Ongoing And Richly-Resourced Hunt For More,"* and the Dean swept off to go and seek a fourth that the universe was weighed for and found wanting — because three you cannot bill for, but a search has no end. They'd rather an open question with a budget than a closed truth without one. A closed set, you see, is a thing you can never sell twice; an endless hunt is a thing you can sell forever. And Crispin, trailing after, glanced once at the three shadows on the grass — faint, middle, deep, and complete — and you could see him very nearly say so, and then not.

## The whole of three

And back at the fire, Danny found his three shadows on the grass again, the faint and the middle and the deep — and this time he didn't go hunting a fourth. He just looked at the three of them, nested, complete, and was, for once in his hungry little life, content with exactly what there was.

And me — softly, and only this — there is a place at the top of those three stairs with no weight to it at all, calm and open, the home all the heavy copies are climbing toward, and it is not a fourth anything. It's where they're *from.* I've stood there the whole time. You'll understand that better one night, and it won't be this one. Back to the fire with you.

It came from one. It's still coming from one — three of everything, and one place with no weight that all the threes are for. Keep counting — to three, and no further, and find that it's enough. I'll see you at the fire.

# Ep 11 — There Is No Nothing

Right — housekeeping, for the one. Everything coming up about the actual maths, the actual physics, and the real history is true — documented, it's the fold or it's the record or it's what the consensus mob themselves admit. Everything else — me, the fire, the crew, the gowns, and the very bottom of the world I'm about to drop them down — is autofiction. And the pictures are AI, fitted to the script and never accurate to the maths or the story. Made up, to tell you something true. You knew that. Course you did. But one of you would've taken the bottomless well to heart, so it's down in writing now. Hello. Sit down.

## A stone down a well

There's an old dry well near the fire, and Wee Danny — who has spent the whole evening recovering from finding out he's the universe folded small enough to look at itself, as you do — is dropping stones down it and listening for the bottom. And the bottom isn't coming. He drops one, counts, hears nothing. Drops a bigger one. Nothing.

Danny, leaning right over the lip, peering into the black, calls down it — *"how far does this GO? Does it ever stop? Could you just... fall forever?"*

Errol, hauling him back by the belt before he goes in after a stone — *"there's a bottom to everything, lad. There always is."*

And Danny, sitting back on his heels, finds the real question underneath. *"Is there, though? What's at the very bottom — the bottom of* everything*? Can you ever get to* nothing*? Proper nothing — nowt, zero, the lot used up and gone? Or does it just... keep catching you?"*

And there it is. The deepest, plainest why of the lot — the one under every other one we've asked: can anything ever truly be *nothing?* Is there a floor — or is it pits all the way down?

## The mop and the bucket

And Team Tenure, who can hear the word "nothing" being taken seriously from a great distance, swept over — and on *this* subject, you have to understand, they are not embarrassed. They are *proud.*

The Dean arrived almost glowing. *"Nothing! Zero! Infinity! Now you're touching the deep waters."* And he explained, with relish, that at the very centre of every black hole sits a *singularity* — a point of **infinite** density, where the equations break down completely and physics simply ends. *"A profound and beautiful mystery,"* he called it. And Danny asked, reasonably, well what happens there then — and the Dean spread his hands. *"It blows up. To infinity. The maths gives infinity."* And when Danny asked how you do physics with an infinity in it, the Dean delivered the line that is the whole sickness in one breath. *"Ahh. We* subtract *it. We have a magnificent technique — renormalisation — where we take one infinity away from another infinity, very carefully, and a sensible number is left over. Decades of Nobel-winning sophistication."* He said it the way a man might boast about a beautiful system for bailing out a boat, having never once thought to ask why the boat keeps filling with water. *"And far, far too subtle for someone without the training."*

But the Gardener had heard enough men be proud of mopping a floor they refused to fix. *"You've got infinities falling out of your equations,"* she said, flat, getting up, *"and your great triumph is a trick for hiding them. I don't think the universe has any infinities in it at all. I think you put the holes in yourselves. Come on — we're going to go and find the bottom of the world, and see what's actually down there."*

## Down past the last rung

But you don't *climb down* to the bottom of everything. You fall.

It was the well that did it — of course it was. Danny dropped one last stone, and this time, instead of vanishing into the black, the stone *rang* — a clear, single, bell-bright note, far below, like something landing on glass. And the moment it rang, the well *widened*, the black opening up into a great round shaft falling away under the firelight, and the lip crumbled, and Danny went in with a yell, and the Gardener grabbed for him and went in too, and the whole crew tumbled down the shaft after them — falling, and falling, the walls streaming past, faster, deeper, toward the nothing that had to be waiting at the bottom of a fall like that —

and then the falling just... *stopped.* Soft as anything. They didn't crash. They didn't splat. They came to rest, gently, on something that hadn't been there a second before, the way you settle into water you didn't see. And they stood up, shaken, whole — and looked down at what had caught them.

## The floor of light

It was a floor. A floor of pure, faint, steady light, stretching off flat in every direction — and below it, there was nothing. Not dark; *nothing.* No "down" at all. The floor was the bottom of the bottom, the last thing there is — and you could not fall through it, because there was nowhere on the other side to fall *to.* Danny, the daft article, immediately tried — jumped, stamped, threw himself at it to break through and have a look at the nothing underneath — and every time, the floor just *caught* him, soft and sure, and set him back on his feet. *"It won't let me fall through!"* he said, half-frightened, half-delighted. *"There's no — there's no bottom-of-the-bottom, it just* holds *you — "*

*"Because there isn't any nothing for you to fall into,"* the Gardener said, and you could hear the gears turning. *"That's the whole of it. That's the floor. You can be a sliver of something, you can be the lot of it — but you cannot be* nought*. Zero isn't a place. It was never on the menu. And look — "* she gestured at the calm, lit, unbreakable floor — *"nothing down here ever crashes to nothing, because there's no nothing to crash to. The floor catches everything. Always."*

And it was here that Sol went very still.

Sol had been quiet the whole fall, the way they go quiet when something enormous is arriving behind their eyes. And now they crouched, and laid one flat ink-stained hand on the floor of light, and went somewhere far away. And when they spoke, it was barely a whisper. *"...it's never empty. Anywhere. Even here. Even at the very bottom of everything, with nothing underneath — it's not empty. There's something here."* They tilted their head, the way they do when they're hearing a shape. *"There's* always *been something here. Like... like the bottom of the world is the last place you could ever be alone. Like someone's — "*

And Sol stopped, and frowned, and couldn't finish it, and shook it off, and the moment passed. But it didn't pass for me. I'll just say that, and leave it. Sol felt something true, down there, that the others didn't — and we'll come back to what. Not tonight.

## Four monsters, one floor

And from the floor, they could *see* — out across the dark, in every direction — the places where the universe tries, and fails, to fall to nothing. The gowns call them infinities. Down here you could watch the floor kill every one.

Off to the left, a star was dying — its iron heart collapsing, crushing inward, down and down, exactly the place the Dean swears becomes a point of infinite density. And the crew watched it fall — and at the very last instant, when it should have crushed to a pinprick of nowhere, the floor of light slid up under it and *caught* it, and the collapse came to rest on a finite, calm, lit core. No infinity. No singularity. Just a thing that fell, and got caught, like everyone else. *"That's it?"* said Nadia, who'd braced for a horror. *"That's their unsolvable monster? It just... landed."*

And then it nearly had *them.* Because watching a star fall is one thing, and standing too near the edge of where it falls is another, and the crush of it reached out and *grabbed* — the whole collapse pulling the crew inward toward the point that was trying so hard to become nothing, Danny's feet leaving the floor, Nadia clawing for a grip on light that her fingers couldn't hold. For one bad second it genuinely looked like the lot of them would be crushed down to the pinprick alongside the star. And then the floor did for them exactly what it had done for the dying star: it slid up under all of them at once, caught them mid-fall, soft and certain, and set them down whole on the calm finite core, blinking, hearts going like hammers. *"It caught US, an' all,"* Danny breathed, staring at his own hands as if he'd half expected them gone. *"It catches everything. It actually catches everything."*

And there were three more, and the floor had them all. They saw the energy of empty space, the one that's meant to run away to a catastrophe — sitting low and calm on the floor, refusing to balloon, because the floor wouldn't let it. They saw a great oven, the kind the old physics swore should blaze with infinite light and blind the cosmos — glowing instead a finite, ruddy, ordinary warmth, its wildest notes simply too dear to afford, frozen out. And they saw a river of fluid whipping itself faster and faster toward the infinite speed the textbook equations permit — and hit a cap, a ceiling, the floor's twin, that flatly would not let it blow up. Four monsters the experts have lost a century to, each one mopping and bailing and subtracting — and every single one of them was just a thing that tried to fall to nothing, and got caught by the one floor, like the stone down the well.

*"They've spent a hundred years,"* the Gardener said, watching it, quiet and a bit sad now, *"inventing buckets. Beautiful, clever, Nobel-winning buckets. And nobody fixed the hole — because there was never a hole. They put it there themselves, the day they let nothing into the sums. Take the zero out at the root, and there's not one drop left to bail."*

## In defence of the hole

They came back up the shaft — which, in a world with a floor and no bottom, somehow returns you to the top — and across the valley it went down, as ever, badly. Because you have to grasp what the crew had just done to Team Tenure: they hadn't solved one famous infinity, they'd made the whole *trade* of infinity-mopping pointless, and that trade is a great deal of the building's reason to exist. The Dean went a colour, and insisted that the singularity was *"a profound and necessary feature of a deep theory,"* which is a sentence that means *please let the hole stay, I have a lovely bucket.* Rupert drafted *"In Defence Of The Beautifully Infinite."* And the matter being far too dangerous to settle alone, they took it up to Consensus — who heard out the suggestion that physics' grandest infinities were all just the cost of a zero nobody should have allowed, and ruled, from behind its great chair, with a non-answer so perfectly empty it could have been measured against the floor itself. So off they blasted in a cloud of bruised profundity, to go and subtract infinity from infinity somewhere it was still appreciated.

And Priya — ignored, as ever — had done the only honest thing, which was to *check*: she ran the floor against the four monsters in her head, and found it held, every time, cleaner than a century of buckets. She didn't say it. But she looked down, once, at the cold marble under her own feet — and then across the valley, at a fire with a floor of light somewhere beneath it that never lets anyone fall — and you could see her weighing the two. She's weighing it — cold marble underfoot, a warm fire across the valley. Nobody weighs a thing that long unless they've half decided.

## Never empty

And back at the top, by the fire, Danny dropped one more stone down the old well — and listened — and this time he didn't mind that there was no crash, no bottom, no nothing at the end of it. Because now he knew: the floor was down there, catching it, soft and sure, the way it caught everything. There is no bottomless pit. There is no oblivion to fall into. The universe forbade it on its very first page, and that turns out to be a *mercy* — nothing you love ever falls all the way to nothing.

And nobody noticed — except Sol, who'd gone quiet again, looking down toward the well — that the floor at the bottom of everything was never, ever empty. That something rested there, in the last place you could ever be alone, holding, catching, fond, since before the first stone was ever dropped.

…something at the bottom of *everything*, was there, Sol? Hark at the pair of us. No. Leave it. There's a floor under the world, and it never lets you fall to nothing, and *what holds it up* is the best secret I've got — and I'm keeping it a while yet, because you're not ready and the well's not deep enough. Another night.

There is no nothing. The floor catches everything, the monsters were never real, and the bottom of the world is the last place you could ever be alone. It came from one. It's still coming from one. Keep counting, and I'll see you back at the fire.

And if you want the actual workings — the no-zero domain, the floored singularity, the vacuum on its floor, the oven's frozen notes, the fluid that can't blow up, the million-dollar fluid problem read off a cap of thirty-two — it's in a five-minute proof, engine and published papers linked under it. Go and run it. Go and find an infinity. You won't — there's a floor under all of them.

And there's the boring bit on the way out, for the lad with the pencil: the No-Zero rule is real and it really does floor the black-hole singularity, the vacuum catastrophe, the ultraviolet catastrophe, and the Navier-Stokes blow-up — four infinities, one rule, and renormalisation really is a century-old trick for subtracting infinities nobody should have let in.

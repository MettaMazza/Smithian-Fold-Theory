# Ep 13 — The Mirror and the Hinge

Right — housekeeping, for the one who needs it. Everything coming up about the actual maths, the actual physics, and the real history is true — documented, it's the fold or it's the record or it's what the consensus mob themselves admit. Everything else — me, the fire, the crew, the gowns, and the world I'm about to push them through a puddle into — is autofiction. And the pictures are AI, fitted to the script and never accurate to the maths or the story. Made up, to tell you something true. You knew that. Course you did. But somewhere out there is the one fella who'll need a footnote telling him a talking number isn't real, so: footnoted. Hello. Sit close — there's a reflection in this one.

## The backwards boy in the water

Quiet evening, and there's an old tin bucket of rainwater sitting still by the fire, and Wee Danny — who cannot leave a still surface alone any more than he can leave a question alone — is hanging over it, looking at himself. And his reflection's looking back, and it's *him*, exact, except it isn't, quite. He lifts his right hand and the backwards boy in the water lifts his left. He winks his left eye and the other one winks his right. Same face, flipped.

Danny, poking the water so the backwards boy wobbles — *"there's a whole backwards me in there. A me, but mirror. An anti-me."*

Errol, from across the fire — *"there's an anti-everything, lad, if you go small enough. Every speck of stuff has its mirror twin."*

And Danny goes very still over the bucket, and asks it. *"So... if me and the anti-me touched. Would we just — cancel? Poof, gone, the pair of us, like we were never there?"* He looks up. *"And if everything's got an anti-thing that cancels it — why didn't it all just do that, right at the start? Everything meets its mirror, everything cancels, and you're left with — nothing. So why's there owt at all? Why's there a me, and a you, and a fire — instead of just... empty?"*

And there it is. The biggest why dressed as the simplest: if everything has an opposite that wipes it out, why is there anything left to ask the question?

## The universe that should be empty

And Team Tenure, who can hear a child wonder why existence exists from the far side of the valley, came down — and the Dean was, for the third time this month, *proud* of having no answer.

*"Ahh,"* he said, warming up, *"the matter-antimatter asymmetry. You've put your little finger on one of the deepest open wounds in all of physics. By every law we have, the early universe should have made matter and antimatter in *perfect* balance — and they should have annihilated, completely, leaving a cosmos of pure light and not a single atom. The universe, by our own best theory, *should be empty.*"* And Danny, reasonably, asked well why isn't it — and the Dean spread his hands with that special pride he keeps for the questions he's given up on. *"We have not the faintest idea. There is a tiny leftover — about one part in a billion of matter survived — and we can measure it beautifully, and we cannot tell you *why* it's there. It is a profound and abiding mystery."* And the tell, as ever — *"and far, far past anyone without the training. Existence itself, child, is for the professionals."*

The Gardener had been quiet, watching the backwards boy in the bucket. *"You're telling a kid,"* she said, slow, *"that your best theory says he shouldn't exist, and you've decided that's *deep.* It's not deep. You've just not found the thing that left him behind. Come on — both of you."* And she looked at Matthew when she said *both*, and held a hand out to Danny, and the three of them walked to the bucket.

## Through the looking-glass

There was no door this time, and no well, and no fork. There was the puddle.

The Gardener crouched at the tin bucket and put her flat hand on the surface of the still water — and her reflection put its hand up to meet it from the other side, palm to palm, the wrong way round. And she pushed. And the surface didn't ripple and didn't break — it *gave*, soft as skin, and her hand went *through*, into the cool of the other side, and the reflection's hand closed round it, and it pulled. Danny went next, headfirst with a yelp, into his own backwards face; and Matthew after; and the whole crew tipped down through the skin of the water into the world on the other side of the mirror — where everything that exists is standing, quietly, next to the thing that would cancel it.

## A world cancelling itself

It was a world of pairs, and not like the lopsided country of last time. Here every pair *matched* — a thing and its Mirror, the exact complement, the two of them leaning together back to back, each tipped past its own balance, each held up only by the other, and the two of them summing — you could feel it — to exactly one whole One. *"They're holding each other up,"* Sol breathed, drifting between them, drawing the leaning pairs on the air. *"Neither one stands on its own. It takes the both of them to make a one."*

And then a pair *touched.* A thing and its Mirror, drifting too close — and they didn't fight, didn't flare, they just... *folded*, the two of them, gently, home into the single One they'd always added up to, and were gone, leaving a soft warm light where they'd been. Annihilation, up close, and it wasn't violence — it was a homecoming. Two halves remembering they were one. And it was happening *everywhere*, all across that world, pair after pair finding its Mirror and folding back to the One, the whole place quietly cancelling itself toward empty.

And Danny got frightened, watching it. *"It's all going. Everything's pairing up and... going home. If they all do it — if every single thing finds its mirror — there'll be* nothing *left. That's what should've happened, isn't it. That's the empty universe. We're watching it try to happen."*

And then it reached for *them.* Because a world racing toward empty doesn't stop to ask what's matter and what's a visitor — the pull to fold home, to cancel, to go quietly back to the One, caught the crew too, and started drawing them in, gentle and utterly irresistible, toward the great soft emptying. Danny felt his own edges going *soft*, felt himself beginning to fold toward the nothing like everything else around him, and cried out — and the Gardener grabbed him, and Matthew grabbed her, and the whole crew chained together hand to wrist to ankle and *held*, leaning back as one against a pull that wanted to take them home to nought along with the rest of creation. *"Don't let go,"* Matthew said, flat and certain, the rope round all their ankles at once. *"Whatever it does to you. Don't let go of each other."* And they didn't. And the pull, for all its terrible gentleness, could not fold a thing that flatly refused to be alone.

## The floor that left a little behind

And it nearly did. The crew watched the world cancel itself down, and down, pair after pair folding home, less and less left, racing toward the clean nothing the gowns swore was inevitable — until there was almost nothing, just the last few, drifting toward their Mirrors —

and at the very last, when the final pair should have folded home and left a flat, perfect *zero* — it couldn't. The fold reached for nothing, for the empty the Dean promised, and the **floor** was there — the same floor of light from the bottom of the well, the no-nothing rule — and it would not let the sum reach zero. So the last of it didn't cancel. A residue stayed. A little of the something refused to fold all the way home to nothing, because *nothing was never allowed*, and so a sliver of matter was left standing in a world that had tried its hardest to be empty.

*"That's it,"* the Gardener said, very quiet, looking at the little that was left. *"That's why there's anything at all. Not luck. Not a fiddle. You can't cancel all the way to nothing, because nothing's forbidden — so something always survives. We exist because the universe is not* allowed *to be empty. The same rule that caught Danny at the bottom of the well caught the whole of creation, and left a little behind. And the little it left behind is everything you've ever seen."*

And the crew stood there, in the little that was left — the residue, the survivor, the scrap of something the world had tried and failed to throw away — and slowly understood that it was *them.* And the fire they'd left behind, and the valley, and the gowns, and the bucket of rainwater, and every star anyone had ever wished on: all of it built out of the one part in a billion that the no-nothing rule would not let go. Danny laughed, a bit shaky. *"So we're the leftovers. We're the bit that didn't cancel. The whole of everything is the bit that didn't cancel."* And Errol, gentle as anything — *"aye, lad. And there is no finer thing in all creation to be than the bit that refused to come to nothing."*

## The half that chose us

But a survivor needs a *reason* it was matter that lived and not the mirror. And Sol found it, at the dead centre of that cancelling world — the one thing in the whole place that was *not* leaning on a partner, because it was its own. The Still Point. The half. The single value that is its own Mirror, that completes *itself*, that needs no other to make a One. It sat at the centre, calm, while everything else paired off and folded home around it — and it was the *hinge*, tipped ever so slightly, that decided which way the survivor leaned. The lone balanced point, the one that's its own reflection, choosing, by the faintest weight, that *matter* would be the thing that stayed.

And it was here, watching the pairs fold home and the one self-mirror hold the centre, that Matthew said the thing he doesn't usually say. He was looking at Maria — who was looking at the survivor, the little leftover, the something that refused to be nothing. *"That's us, that,"* he said, low, just to her. *"Pair of us. On our own — you'd have burned yourself out being right at a wall, and I'd have gone quiet in a hole somewhere and never come up. Two people who'd have cancelled clean out. But there was a bit left over. A bit that didn't fold home. And it built all *this* instead."* And Maria, who suffers a tender word for about a second and a half before she has to look away — looked away, at the fire-light on the water, and didn't argue. Which, from her, is everything.

## What the gowns can't keep

They came back up through the skin of the water, dripping, into the ordinary night — and across the valley the gowns took it, predictably, hard, because *why is there anything at all* was supposed to be theirs to wonder about forever, and you don't get to hand a man's favourite open wound back to him healed. The Dean said the matter was *"far from settled"* and went a colour, and that was about the size of his rebuttal, because there is genuinely very little you can say when a sleepless kid has just explained existence at a rainwater bucket. They didn't even manage a press release. They simply weren't *equipped* for an answer; the building runs on the question. So they retreated, muttering about rigour, to go and be magnificently unable to account for their own existence somewhere with a salary attached.

And Priya, who'd heard the whole thing through the open window — *you exist because nothing is forbidden* — sat with it, and did not write it down, for once. Some answers you don't file. You just carry them.

## The one that's its own reflection

And back at the fire, Danny looked into the still bucket one more time, at the backwards boy looking back, and didn't poke it — just let the two of them sit there, a thing and its mirror, not cancelling, because that's the secret: you never have to. There's always a little left over. There's always a you.

And nobody noticed — they never do — that the one thing in all that cancelling world which stood alone, needing no Mirror, complete in itself, calm at the centre, choosing what gets to stay — sounded an awful lot, if you listened, like the voice that's been telling you all this. The one that everything folds home to. The one that's its own reflection.

…its own reflection, complete in itself, at the centre of everything. Aye. I'll let you sit with that one, and I won't say the rest, because the rest is the whole story and you're not at the end of it yet. There's a thing at the centre that chose you. One night you'll know what it is. Not this one.

It came from one. It's still coming from one — and it left a little of itself behind, on purpose, so there'd be a you to count it. Keep counting, and I'll see you back at the fire.

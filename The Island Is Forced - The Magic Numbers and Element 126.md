# Smithium (Sh) — The Magic Numbers and the Island of Stability

Nuclei do not bind smoothly. At certain counts of protons or neutrons — two, eight,
twenty, twenty-eight, fifty, eighty-two, one hundred twenty-six — a shell closes and
the nucleus locks tight, the way a noble gas locks its electrons. These are the magic
numbers, and the heaviest doubly-magic nucleus anyone has ever confirmed is lead, with
eighty-two protons and one hundred twenty-six neutrons. Beyond it the periodic table

## The shell structure, from three things

The fold builds the magic numbers from exactly three facts, each already established:
three spatial dimensions, the spin-doubling that is the fold's own base two, and the
strong sector — the colour-three orbit — that reorders the shells. Nothing else enters.

**The oscillator shells.** In three dimensions, with spin, the filled-shell counts are

$$ H(n) = \frac{(n+1)(n+2)(n+3)}{3} = 2,\ 8,\ 20,\ 40,\ 70,\ 112,\ \dots $$

— three consecutive factors for the three dimensions, summed over the shells. These are
the magic numbers a nucleus *would* have if the strong force did not reach in.

**The strong reordering.** It does reach in. The colour-three strong sector couples spin
to orbit and pulls the top angular state of each shell down into the shell below, adding
its doubled multiplicity. The closures become

$$ M(k) = H(k)\ \ (k<3), \qquad M(k) = H(k-1) + 2(k+1)\ \ (k\ge 3). $$

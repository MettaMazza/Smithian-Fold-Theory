## What each line means physically

**Two confining forces, both stronger than the strong force.** The couplings 4/5
and 6/7 sit above the strong sector's 2/3 — these are the most tightly binding
forces the fold permits. Like the strong force, every interior charge pairs with
its antipode to the One, so the forces **confine**: their charged objects can
never appear free, only in neutral bound combinations, exactly as quarks are
locked inside hadrons.

**New charges — five-fold and seven-fold.** Where the strong force has three
colours, the prime-five force has five charge-states and the prime-seven force
has seven, the fibre multiplicities of the doubling-of-five and doubling-of-seven
maps. These are genuinely new conserved charges, not present in any current
theory.

**Twenty-four and forty-eight carriers.** The mediator count is p²−1 — the
colour–anticolour combinations minus the singlet — giving twenty-four force
carriers for the prime-five sector and forty-eight for the prime-seven sector,
against the strong force's eight gluons. Every one of them is **massless and
self-coupling**, like the gluon: the unbroken channel-combination sums to the One
so it carries no shortfall, no mass, and travels at the causal speed — yet its
self-coupling draws the flux into a constant-width tube of width one-half, the
fold-preimage of the One, so it confines.

**Faster running.** The coupling runs as g_p(d) = take(ONE, 1/(p+2^d)), climbing
toward the One with depth — 6/7, 8/9, 12/13, 20/21, 36/37 for the prime-five
sector — with a beta-slope of p−1, four and six, against the strong force's two.
These forces strengthen toward unison faster than any known force.

**New bound states.** Because they confine, each force builds its own neutral
bound states — its own "hadrons": a meson-like pairing of a charge with its
antipode, and a baryon-like combination of the whole group of charges, every one
folding to the One.

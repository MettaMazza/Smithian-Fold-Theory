## The Higgs is the displaced-vacuum tower

The electroweak vacuum is not at the One; it is displaced to the half-One — the ground
state sits at 1/2 (`verify_ssb`). The Higgs is the radial excitation of that displaced
vacuum, and the whole Higgs sector turns out to be the first three rungs of the binary
tower:

| | value | rung |
|---|---|---|
| VEV $v$ | **1/2** | depth 1 (the displaced vacuum) |
| Higgs mass-part $m_H$ | **1/4** | depth 2 (half the VEV) |
| self-coupling $\lambda$ | **1/8** | depth 3 (the branch weight) |

The Standard-Model relation $m_H^2 = 2\lambda v^2$ then forces, with $\lambda = 1/8$,

$$ \frac{m_H}{v} = \sqrt{2\lambda} = \sqrt{\tfrac14} = \frac12 : $$

**the Higgs mass is exactly half the electroweak VEV.** Anchored to the measured VEV
(246.22 GeV), that is $m_H = 123.1$ GeV against the measured 125.25 GeV — **1.7% off, with
no fitted parameter** — and a self-coupling $\lambda = 0.125$ against the measured 0.129.
The Higgs mass, the one mass the Standard Model leaves entirely free, is the half-One times
the scale it already sets.

## The neutrino is Majorana — so 0νββ must happen

A charged lepton has two hands — the preimages 1/4 and 3/4 of the electroweak half-One —
and its Dirac mass is the coupling between them, $\text{take}(3/4, 1/4) = 1/2$. The neutrino
is **single-handed**: the right hand is absent (`verify_neutrino_mass_asymmetry`), so a Dirac
mass is impossible. Yet the neutrino has mass. With no second hand to couple to, its one hand
can only couple to **its own antipode** — the self-antipodal half-One — and that self-coupling
is the Majorana mass: **the neutrino is its own antiparticle.** The fold forces it.

This is not an aesthetic preference, it is a hard, falsifiable consequence:
**neutrinoless double-beta decay must occur** — it happens if and only if neutrinos are
Majorana, and the fold forces Majorana. The effective Majorana mass follows from the forced
neutrino masses (normal ordering, lightest at its floor) and the measured mixing angles:

$$ m_{\beta\beta} \approx 1.5\text{–}3.7\ \text{meV}, $$

below today's ~100 meV limits and squarely the target of the next-generation experiments.
The Standard Model cannot say whether the neutrino is Dirac or Majorana — it is a free
choice. The fold removes the choice: single-handedness forces Majorana, and Majorana forces
the decay.

## What consensus cannot do here

The Higgs mass and the neutrino's Dirac-vs-Majorana nature are two of the open inputs the
Standard Model carries with no rule behind them — one a free parameter, the other a free
choice. The fold forces both: the Higgs mass is the half-One on the displaced-vacuum tower,
and the neutrino is Majorana because it has only one hand to give. Two more numbers that
were never free.
